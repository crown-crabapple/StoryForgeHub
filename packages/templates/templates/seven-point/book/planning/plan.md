## About this structure

Seven-point structure picks seven moments that hold a story up and links them in order. Many writers plan it backwards: decide the resolution first, then choose a hook that sits as far from it as possible, and fill in the points between.

Based on the seven-point story structure taught by Dan Wells.

## How to use it

1. Read through the beats below and note, in your own words, what each one will be in this book.
2. Open each outline scene, replace the prompt with your own draft, and change its status as you go.
3. Add scenes to a chapter, or split and merge chapters, whenever the story asks for it. The structure is a guide, not a rule.

## The beats

### 1. Hook

Open on a state of affairs that is the opposite of where the story will end.

### 2. Plot Turn 1

An event launches the main conflict.

### 3. Pinch 1

Pressure is applied and the protagonist must act.

### 4. Midpoint

The protagonist moves from reacting to acting.

### 5. Pinch 2

Things get worse and all seems lost.

### 6. Plot Turn 2

The protagonist gains the last piece they need.

### 7. Resolution

The conflict is settled and the story ends.
